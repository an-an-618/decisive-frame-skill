## 1. Frame Card

Inspect the source before proposing anything. Resolve:

- **Semantic minimum:** the smallest subject and relationship that still identify this scene.
- **Core subjects:** one or two elements carrying identity, action, or narrative weight.
- **Spatial invariants:** horizon, perspective, relative positions, facing, overlap, path, or silhouette that must survive.
- **Dominant gesture:** gaze, lean, diagonal, curve, convergence, repetition, or movement.
- **Visual-weight map:** area, darkness, saturation, faces, isolation, texture, and edge tension.
- **Native color map:** dominant hue families plus small saturated regions with narrative potential.
- **Quiet zones:** sky, fog, water, wall, ground, reflection, shadow, or low-information space.
- **16:9 risk:** what a horizontal reframe could crop, enlarge, stretch, duplicate, or falsely invent.
- **Discard list:** repeated texture, clutter, redundant objects, and minor description that should disappear.
- **Abstraction map:** name what must remain as structure, what collapses into one wash, what disappears entirely, and where clean white ground must open.
- **Support opportunity:** identify one nearby rail, edge, shoreline, platform, shadow, or quiet wash that could deform into a local cradle behind the anchor without becoming a new object.
- **Human scale:** when a person may become the anchor, estimate final figure height and record the identity carriers that survive abstraction: pose, head angle, proportions, clothing silhouette, hair mass, and distinctive accessories.

Treat color and subject selection as a hierarchy decision, not object detection alone.

## 2. Mode Gate

Use this order:

```text
At least one valid element exists after 16:9 recomposition
  → Decisive Element Mode

No valid element exists
  → Decisive Color Mode
```

Choose the mode once per photo. All candidates for that photo stay in the chosen mode. Never offer a mixed set and make the user decide the mode.

## 3. Decisive Element Mode

An element is valid only when it passes every gate:

1. **Semantic weight:** removing it would weaken scene meaning or eye path.
2. **Legibility:** its silhouette, gesture, or identity reads independently.
3. **Isolation:** color can remain local without leaking through half the frame.
4. **Final-frame scale:** it stays below 50% after 16:9 recomposition.
5. **Negative-space leverage:** the monochrome world can breathe around it.

Area guidance:

| Final-frame share | Decision |
|---:|---|
| 5–30% | Preferred |
| 30–45% | Exceptional; require an unusually strong silhouette or spatial relationship |
| 45–49% | Usually reject; accept only with unmistakable surrounding negative space |
| 50% or more | Hard reject |

Reject large environmental masses such as an entire sky, sea, mountain, forest, façade, room, or table spread. A large mass may remain part of the monochrome world but cannot be the colored element.

Preserve the approved element's internal light, material, and volume. “Keep the element colored” never means replace it with a flat sticker or complete outline.

### Human anchors

A person is valid because of gesture, silhouette, action, relationship, or presence—not because image generation can reproduce a photographic face. When a person is selected:

- preserve source pose, head direction, shoulder–hip relationship, limb count, joint locations, body proportions, clothing silhouette, hair mass, and distinctive carried items;
- treat exact facial likeness as intentionally abstracted rather than falsely reconstructed;
- render the whole figure in one coherent line-and-wash language, not a realistic face pasted onto a painted body;
- retain source-derived color as translucent wash within the figure, never as a flat vector fill;
- simplify hands and feet according to scale while preserving their count, placement, and gesture;
- reject cartoon, chibi, mannequin, beauty-filter, fashion-illustration idealization, or invented anatomy.

Use final-frame figure height to select the facial abstraction tier:

| Figure height | Face treatment |
|---:|---|
| Above 20% | Five to nine broken graphite or charcoal marks for brow line, eye direction, nose bridge, mouth interval, jaw, and hair boundary |
| 8–20% | Three to six economical marks; preserve head angle and expression direction, not eye-level detail |
| Below 8% | One to three gestural marks or a blank face plane; do not invent miniature features |

Non-human anchors remain material-faithful and do not use this branch.

## 4. Decisive Color Mode

Use only when no valid element passes the gate.

Choose one source hue tied to a local region that performs at least one job:

- establishes the focal entry;
- redirects the eye;
- creates a pause or counterweight;
- clarifies depth or figure–ground;
- carries a scene-specific emotion or material fact.

Retain only the useful local occurrence. Convert all other matching or neighboring hues to neutral ink. Examples:

- keep one lit window, not every warm light;
- keep one strip of turquoise tide, not the whole sea;
- keep one colored counterform in a meal, not all food of that hue;
- keep one vegetation opening, not an entire green cliff.

Target roughly 2–18% of the final frame. Treat 25% as a warning threshold, not a goal. A coherent nearby cluster may count as one region; widely scattered echoes do not.

Apply the removal test: mentally remove the color. If balance, movement, figure–ground, or meaning barely changes, choose again.

## 5. Candidate Diversity

Return two or three credible proposals. Candidates must differ meaningfully in at least two of these dimensions:

- anchor or local color region;
- 16:9 crop and subject placement;
- direction of breathing room;
- ink-weight distribution;
- amount and direction of contiguous white field;
- anchor-support family and its relationship to nearby scene geometry;
- anchor rendering branch and, for a person, facial abstraction tier;
- eye path;
- emotional proposition.

When one anchor is clearly superior, keep it and vary the framing, interval, or ink world. Do not manufacture an inferior second subject.

Each proposal must state Mode, Anchor, Why, Color extent, 16:9 direction, Ink translation, Anchor support, Anchor rendering, and Emotional proposition.

## 6. Selection Mistakes

| Failure | Correction |
|---|---|
| “Most colorful object” selected automatically | Re-evaluate semantic weight and eye path |
| Large sky, sea, cliff, or façade chosen as element | Reject by final-frame area; test a local color region |
| Every similar hue retained | Keep only the narratively useful occurrence |
| Multiple people or objects colored | Reduce to one inseparable subject or switch mode |
| Candidates are cosmetic variants | Change crop, interval, ink hierarchy, or proposition |
| Weak candidate added to reach three | Return two credible candidates |
| Every source detail survives in gray | Keep only defining structure, merge secondary masses, and omit the rest into white |
| Anchor disappears into pale ink | Add one local light-to-mid support derived from nearby geometry or a shaped wash |
| Support becomes a black blob or hard panel | Lighten it, reduce its area, and dissolve its edge; keep the anchor fully legible |
| Same generic splash used for every photo | Derive the support from the anchor gesture or an adjacent source structure |
| Human face regenerated photorealistically | Convert the entire person to one coherent scale-aware line-and-wash figure |
| Detailed face invented on a small figure | Reduce it to the permitted facial marks or a blank face plane |
| Human becomes a generic cartoon or mannequin | Restore source pose, proportions, clothing silhouette, hair mass, and carried items |
| Human line art loses the decisive color | Reintroduce restrained source-color wash inside the figure only |
